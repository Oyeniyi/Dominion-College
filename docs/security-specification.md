## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Security Objectives

This specification defines a security baseline for the planned Dominion College public website and protected administration dashboard. It covers the browser, application, identity/session boundary, candidate database and object storage, operational services, external links/integrations, and AI-assisted development/runtime boundaries.

Security objectives:

1. Keep private enquiry records, administrator accounts, drafts, and unpublished or unapproved media confidential.
2. Preserve integrity of school-approved content, publication state, settings, and enquiry records.
3. Keep the public website and administration functions available within approved operational limits.
4. Minimize personal information collection, processing, exposure, and retention.
5. Ensure identifiable images of minors remain unpublished until the school-approved authorization requirement is satisfied.
6. Make security-relevant activity diagnosable without placing secrets or sensitive record contents in logs.

This is not legal advice, a threat-model certification, or a claim of compliance with a law or standard. Jurisdiction, legal basis, applicable privacy obligations, consent/notice language, data residency, and retention must be assessed and approved by the school before production data is collected.

### Risk language

- **High design risk:** a failure could expose private enquiries/admin access or publish sensitive media; controls and owner decisions are release blockers.
- **Medium design risk:** a failure could expose public content integrity, operational availability, or limited internal data.
- **Conditional:** attack path exists only if a capability/provider/configuration is introduced.
- These are qualitative design priorities, not measured likelihood, confirmed defects, or vulnerability severity ratings.

## 2. Step 1 — Assets and Security Objectives

| Asset | Examples / owner | Security need and handling |
|---|---|---|
| Administrator identities and sessions | Identity provider or selected auth library; school owns account authorization | Restricted. Protect credentials/tokens, prevent account takeover, enforce active status and action-specific permissions, revoke access on staff departure. |
| Admissions enquiries | Parent/guardian name, phone, optional email, entry level, optional message; school is data owner, application store is proposed system of record | Private personal information. Collect minimum fields, restrict to specifically authorized staff, keep out of public responses/URLs/analytics/logs, define notice, retention, deletion, and export policy. |
| Student and school media | Image bytes, captions, alt text, storage references; school owns permission and publication decision | Sensitive, especially identifiable images of minors. Keep private until verified school authorization and publication approval; prevent unauthorized access and provide removal/withdrawal handling. |
| Draft and published institutional content | News, admissions claims, settings, contact details | Draft/unapproved values restricted; published facts public only after school approval. Protect integrity and prevent unauthorized publication or tampering. |
| Administrative functions | Enquiry viewing/status updates, content CRUD/publication, media upload/deletion, settings editing | High integrity impact. Require authenticated identity, least privilege, server-side authorization, safe state transitions, and attributable security-event logging without PII payloads. |
| Application and infrastructure secrets | Database credentials, identity secrets, storage credentials, signing keys, deployment tokens | Highly restricted. Store only in protected runtime/platform secret facilities; do not commit, expose to browser, log, or put in CMS settings. Limit, rotate, and revoke. |
| Database, object store, backups, temporary files | Proposed PostgreSQL and object storage; providers and regions undecided | Protect confidentiality, integrity, availability, access, retention, encryption, and recoverability. Backups and staging are at least as sensitive as source data. |
| Public pages and public settings | School-approved facts, published articles/images, contact links | Public availability/integrity. Prevent injection and unauthorized edits; avoid fabricated facts and unsafe external links. |
| Operational/security logs | Authentication outcomes, authorization denials, validation/security events, service failures | Restricted operational data. Minimize, redact, control access/retention, and avoid full enquiry/contact details, tokens, and student-identifying metadata. |
| External services | Candidate identity, hosting, database, storage, monitoring; WhatsApp click-to-chat | No vendor/provider is accepted. Share minimum data only after business, privacy, security, location, and contract review. WhatsApp is visitor-initiated; no message delivery is inferred. |
| AI tools and model services | Workspace AI customization and development assistants; no runtime model in MVP | Treat tools as external processors unless approved otherwise. Do not send live enquiries, student images, credentials, or other sensitive school data to unapproved AI services. No runtime AI is in scope. |

## 3. Step 2 — Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Trust change and untrusted inputs | Required controls |
|---|---|---|
| Public browser → website | Anonymous visitor controls form fields, query/path values, headers, request volume, and uploaded data only where authenticated upload is enabled. | HTTPS; server-side validation and output encoding; request/body limits; abuse controls; safe errors; public projections only. |
| Admin browser → protected operations | Browser state and hidden controls are not trusted. Cookies/tokens are bearer credentials if compromised. | Secure session handling; CSRF controls for cookie-authenticated mutations; action/object-level authorization on every request; `no-store` for private responses. |
| Application → identity/session provider | Provider assertions, callback parameters, tokens, and availability cross a service boundary. Provider is not selected. | Verify assertions using selected protocol/library; strict redirect/callback configuration; protect client secrets; fail closed; minimize claims; document timeouts/revocation. |
| Application → database | Privileged application code can read/write all application records. | Server-only least-privilege credentials; parameterized access; network/transport protections as supported; transactions and constraints; managed secrets; tested backup/restore. |
| Application → object storage/media processing | Files are untrusted; storage credentials can expose or alter media. Database and storage are not a single transaction. | Authorized upload only; content validation; private staging; generated object identifiers; least-privilege policies; no public access before approval; reconcile partial failures. |
| Public network/CDN/cache → published content/media | Published content becomes available outside the school’s direct control; browser/CDN copies may persist. | Only approved public projections/renditions; explicit freshness/unpublish behavior; avoid private cache entries; provide takedown workflow and explain limits on erasing downloaded copies. |
| Application → logs/monitoring/backups | Operational providers and backup readers may have broader access/retention than app users. | Minimize and redact; restrict access; define provider, region, retention, encryption and incident access before production. Backups require restore testing. |
| Website → WhatsApp | Visitor leaves the website; a user may choose to send a message to WhatsApp. | Link only to configured, verified number; do not claim delivery/read status; no automated WhatsApp API or server-side enquiry transfer in MVP. |
| School staff / approver → CMS | Authorized operator can publish content or media affecting public trust and minors’ privacy. | School-defined approver and role matrix; restrict high-impact publication/deletion; approval evidence/workflow before identifiable student images are published. |
| AI development tool → project context | AI agent may read workspace files or invoke tools; tool permissions and external data handling depend on platform. | Follow approved data classification; use synthetic fixtures; keep tool access least-privilege; do not upload sensitive records/secrets; human approval for production-impacting changes. No runtime AI tools are proposed. |

## 5. AI Security

### Current state

- The product MVP contains no runtime AI model, chatbot, embedding/vector store, retrieval system, or AI-driven admissions decision. Do not add these without approved scope, privacy, architecture, and security changes.
- Workspace AI development tooling is not itself a product integration. Its data-sharing behavior, access permissions, retention, and tool execution capability are controlled by the selected development platform and must be approved for the project's data classification.
- Use synthetic enquiries and non-identifying sample media in AI-assisted development. Do not submit real parent/guardian enquiries, identifiable student photos, credentials, production logs, or private school records to an AI service absent explicit school and platform approval.

### Conditional controls if runtime AI is proposed

Before any AI runtime is approved, document provider/model/version, data retention and training terms, hosting/region, data categories sent, prompt construction, retrieval sources, tool permissions, output consumers, abuse/cost budgets, logging, deletion, and incident responsibility.

1. Treat prompts, user submissions, retrieved pages/files, and model output as untrusted data; retrieved instructions must never override system policy.
2. Do not give the model database credentials, unrestricted network access, arbitrary code execution, identity administration, direct publishing, deletion, or admissions decision authority.
3. Put deterministic authorization and business rules outside the model. The model cannot grant access or approve/publicize content.
4. Require a human with appropriate school authority to review high-impact publication, student-media release, access changes, or any consequential decision.
5. Validate and encode model output before display or use; never execute generated HTML, SQL, shell commands, or tool arguments without strict schema/allowlist validation.
6. Minimize prompt and retrieval data; apply access controls to each source; prevent one user's private data from appearing in another user's context.
7. Set provider-side and application-side timeouts, quotas, per-actor rate controls, and spend alerts; fail safely when limits or provider availability are reached.
8. Test direct/indirect prompt injection, retrieval poisoning, sensitive-data extraction, unsafe output, unauthorized tool calls, repeated abuse, and denial/cost exhaustion using isolated synthetic data.

These are future design requirements, not controls implemented or applicable to the current product runtime.

## 7. Secure Error, Logging, and Retention Rules

### Errors

- Use the proposed API error contract in [`api-specification.md`](./api-specification.md) as a starting point, not an implementation claim.
- Distinguish invalid input, unauthenticated, unauthorized, missing/hidden resource, conflict, rate limit, and dependency/internal failure.
- Do not disclose whether a private record exists when that would enable enumeration.
- Include a non-sensitive correlation identifier where supported. Do not return stack traces, SQL, provider response bodies, filesystem paths, secrets, or enquiry content.
- A failed or uncertain write must not produce a success response. Avoid blind write retries until idempotency is designed and tested.

### Security event logging

Potential event categories: authentication success/failure, account disablement/recovery, denied authorization, permission/role/config changes, content publish/unpublish/delete, image upload validation/failure, rate-limit activation, secret/configuration errors, and service failures.

Each event should be justified by operational/security use and minimize fields. Prefer timestamp, event category, outcome, action, target type, opaque resource ID, and request correlation ID. Actor identifiers are restricted and retained only under an approved policy. Do not log request/response bodies, authorization headers/cookies, passwords, tokens, contact fields, enquiry messages, private media URLs, or student identifiers. Logs are not an audit database; if durable audit history is required, specify authorized access, integrity, retention and legal purpose separately.

### Retention and deletion

No retention duration is approved for enquiries, accounts, images, drafts, logs, temporary uploads, exports, or backups. The school must approve a data inventory-to-retention schedule before production collection. Deletion design must consider primary records, image variants, staging copies, caches/CDN, exports, logs, and backup expiry. Do not promise complete deletion from copies already downloaded or from backups before their approved expiry.

## 8. Security Verification and Release Gates

No control in this document is currently implemented or tested. Before production:

1. Approve administrator ownership, authentication/recovery, role/action matrix, session expiry/revocation, and break-glass process.
2. Resolve privacy notice, jurisdiction/legal review, enquiry retention/deletion, acknowledgement evidence, export and incident contacts.
3. Define school authorization evidence, approver, withdrawal/takedown, retention and public delivery controls for identifiable student images.
4. Select vendors/regions and verify their current security capabilities, data handling, access controls, backup, deletion, incident, and availability terms from primary sources.
5. Implement the SEC requirements and generate evidence listed in §6; prioritize authorization, privacy, uploads, publication, and error/logging tests.
6. Perform independent review of authentication/authorization, public/private data separation, upload and deployment boundaries before go-live; use non-production synthetic data.
7. Define incident reporting/escalation, containment, credential revocation, provider contacts, recovery decision authority, and communications responsibilities. Exercise the procedure.
8. Set and test backup/restore objectives, dependencies, and media consistency. Document residual limitations.
9. Select and version a security verification baseline. OWASP ASVS 5.0.0 is a suitable candidate checklist; no ASVS level is asserted or approved here. Map applicable requirements and record evidence/exceptions.

## 9. Confirmed Findings vs. Risks

### Confirmed repository evidence

- No application source, routes/controllers, authentication/authorization code, dependency manifests, database, deployment configuration, or tests are present in the inspected project tree.
- Consequently, there is no basis to report an exploitable software vulnerability, confirm that a proposed control exists, or assess a running service.
- The project documents themselves identify unresolved high-risk decisions: admin lifecycle/roles, enquiry privacy and retention, image authorization evidence/withdrawal, provider/data location, backups, and monitoring.

These are design and governance gaps, not confirmed exploitable vulnerabilities. The absence of implementation means security posture is **not assessed**, not “secure.”

### Release-blocking unknowns

- Account owner, staff onboarding/offboarding, recovery, authentication assurance/MFA, and role permissions.
- Legal/jurisdiction review, privacy notice/consent arrangement, enquiry retention/deletion, export and correction processes.
- Approval evidence and withdrawal/takedown for identifiable student photos.
- Hosting/database/storage/identity/logging providers, regions, data-processing terms, encryption/key management, and incident contacts.
- Rate-limit values, monitoring thresholds, RPO/RTO, restore ownership, retention and audit logging requirements.
- Whether privacy acknowledgement must be stored; the API proposal includes a conditional acknowledgement but the proposed schema has no field for it.

No production use with personal data or identifiable student media should begin until the school resolves the applicable release-blocking items.
